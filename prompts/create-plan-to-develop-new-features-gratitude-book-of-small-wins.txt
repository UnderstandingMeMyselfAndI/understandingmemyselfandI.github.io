2. FEATURES TO PLAN
A. The Little Book of Small Wins
Source content (verbatim from product brief):
“Celebrating small wins in addiction recovery means acknowledging tiny, daily victories that retrain your brain's reward system and create positive momentum for long-term sobriety.
Recovery is not just one massive choice. It is built on thousands of small, unglamorous decisions made every single day.
Many of us became experts at self-criticism and inflicting ourselves with harmful thoughts but not so good at accepting praise and taking complements.
The little book of small wins allows us to record the little, but effective, small wins that we achieve, and often forget on our journey.
Make a note of your small wins today and help your future self, when you need a reminder of how far you have come.
Why Small Wins Matter
• Rebuilds the Brain: Addiction changes how your brain handles dopamine. Acknowledging a small success releases natural dopamine, helping your brain heal and learn to enjoy healthy choices again.
• Stops All-or-Nothing Thinking: People often feel like they are failing if they are not perfect. Small wins remind you that progress happens in steps, not giant leaps.
• Creates Confidence: A track record of tiny successes gives you proof that you can change. This builds the self-esteem you need to tackle bigger goals later.
Examples of Small Wins
• Waking up clear-headed without regret or physical pain.
• Resisting a single craving, even for just a few minutes.
• Attending a support meeting or calling a counselor.
• Doing a basic daily task like making your bed or eating a healthy meal.
• Expressing your true feelings honestly instead of hiding them.
How to Practice Small Wins
• Notice the moment: Pause and tell yourself that what you just did matters.
• Write it down: Keep a simple journal to track your daily victories.
• Share your success: Tell a trusted friend, family member, or peer group about your progress.
• Be kind to yourself: Read more about how celebrating small wins strengthens your recovery to build self-compassion.”
Additional product requirements for this feature:

User can record a small successful event.
All data inputs must have title text above the input / textarea / date-picker field.
“Who with” field: people’s names are stored separately so they can be re-used.
As the user types in a “who with” input, previously saved names that match the typed characters must be suggested (type-ahead / autocomplete).
