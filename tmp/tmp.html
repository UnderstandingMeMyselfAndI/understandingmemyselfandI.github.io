DEAR MAN is what you use when you need to ask for something, set a boundary, or tell someone "no" — and your gremlin\'s already lining up excuses to cave.

It\'s a DBT tool. Dialectical Behaviour Therapy. Fancy name. But underneath? It\'s just a script for keeping your spine straight when the pressure\'s on.

Because here\'s the thing: when you\'re in recovery, or just trying to hold your life together, you need to be able to ask for what you need and shut down what you don\'t. But your gremlin? It wants you to stay small. Apologise. Say yes when you mean no. Fold.

DEAR MAN gives you the lines. You just deliver them.

Why this one\'s a keeper

Because when you\'re in the middle of it — when someone\'s pushing, when you\'re about to say yes to something you\'ll regret, when you need to ask for help but your throat closes up — you forget. You forget you\'re allowed to ask. You forget you\'re allowed to say no.

DEAR MAN is a prompt. A reminder. A structure you can lean on when your own backbone\'s feeling wobbly.

The street-level truth

It feels weird at first. Scripting conversations. Practising like you\'re in a play.

Then you use it once. Twice. And you realise: this actually works. People don\'t walk over you as much. You get more of what you need. You say no and the world doesn\'t end.

And that feeling — of holding your ground, clean and calm, without the fallout — that\'s gold.

  • When you need to ask for something but your throat closes up

  • When someone\'s pushing your buttons and you need to set a boundary

  • When you\'re about to say yes to something you\'ll regret

  • When you need to tell someone no but you\'re scared of the fallout

  • When you\'re in a conflict and you need to stay calm and clear
